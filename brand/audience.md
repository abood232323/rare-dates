# Target Audience — Rare Dates

Reads from `context.md` + `decisions.md`. Market: Jordan, Amman-led. Channel per **D3**:
catalogue site → WhatsApp order. Confidence marks as in `context.md`.

> **Verification note.** This framework is built from category and market reasoning, not from
> interviews — there are no customers yet. Every persona is a falsifiable hypothesis. The
> cheapest way to validate: the client already has 55 followers who chose to follow a brand

## 01 — Audience Overview

Rare Dates is not bought to be eaten. It is bought to be **handed to someone**.

The buyer is a Jordanian, mostly in West Amman, in a life stage crowded with obligation:
Ramadan iftars at someone else's house, an engagement, a new baby, a colleague's promotion, a
condolence visit, a relative arriving from the Gulf. Each of these requires arriving with
something. The something is almost always dates, chocolate, or sweets — and almost always the
same three shops.

The frustration is not price and not quality. It is that **the gift says nothing**. Handing over
a familiar box communicates that the obligation was met, not that the person was thought about.
What this buyer wants is a gift that is unmistakably considered — recognisably premium, visibly
different from what everyone else brought, and still legible as *ours*, not an imported luxury
that reads as showing off.

That is a positioning gap, not a product gap. The date is not the product. **The moment of
handing it over is the product.**

## 02 — Primary Audience Profile

### Demographics
- **Age**: 27–55, with the volume centre at 30–45
- **Gender**: skews female for personal/occasion gifting (~65/35); skews male for corporate
- **Location**: West Amman — Abdoun, Deir Ghbar, Khalda, Um Uthaina, Sweifieh, Dabouq;
  secondary Jabal Amman / Weibdeh professional-creative cluster; tertiary Irbid and Aqaba
  affluent pockets, plus Jordanian diaspora in the Gulf
- **Income**: household JOD 1,500+/month; comfortable at JOD 20–45 for a gift box without
  needing to justify it `[I — price point unconfirmed]`
- **Life stage**: established professionals, young families, business owners

### Psychographics
- **Values**: generosity as identity, not etiquette. In Jordan, hospitality is self-definition.
  How you host and what you arrive with *is* a statement about who you are.
- **Aspiration**: to be the person with taste. Not the richest in the room — the one who found
  something no one else knew about.
- **Self-identity**: modern and Arab at once, with no contradiction between the two. Drinks
  specialty coffee, follows Jordanian designers, and still does the family Friday lunch.
  Rejects both the dusty-traditional and the try-hard-Western.
- **Brands they already love**: specialty coffee roasters in Jabal Amman, Jordanian jewellery
  and ceramics labels, Bateel when travelling through the Gulf, Aesop, Patchi under duress.
  Common thread: **restraint, and a story that holds up when asked about it.**
- **Content**: Instagram first and effectively only. Reels, saved posts, Stories. Food and
  interiors accounts. WhatsApp family and work groups are the real distribution layer.
- **Communities**: extended family groups, work groups, university cohorts, neighbourhood
  parent groups. Recommendations move through these faster than through any advertising.

### Behavioural patterns
- **Decision style**: fast, visual, socially validated. The decision is made inside Instagram in
  under two minutes — *does this look expensive, do I recognise it, will it embarrass me.*
- **Trigger**: an event on the calendar or a sudden obligation. Almost never "I feel like dates."
  Demand is **event-driven and violently seasonal** — Ramadan and the two Eids are the year.
- **Discovery**: Instagram Explore, Stories reposts, a friend's WhatsApp forward. Not Google, not
  paid search. A single repost from an Amman food account outperforms a month of posting.
- **Buying process**: sees post → checks the grid to confirm the brand is real → looks for a
  price → DMs or taps through to WhatsApp → asks *how much, what's inside, can you deliver
  Thursday* → pays cash on delivery or CliQ. **This is exactly why D3 is correct** — the buyer
  already expects to talk to a person. A checkout would be the unfamiliar path, not the easy one.
- **Hesitation**: no visible price, slow reply, unclear delivery date, an account that looks
  abandoned. Any one of these ends it. **Reply speed is a brand attribute in this market.**

## 03 — Personas

### Layan, 31 — Marketing Manager, Khalda, Amman

> *"I don't want to show up with the same box everyone brings. I want them to ask me where I got it."*

**Her world:** Works at an agency in Shmeisani, drives to a family gathering most weekends, and
has three group chats actively planning something. She has an occasion roughly twice a month
that requires arriving with something. She has a mental shortlist of two shops and is bored of
both.

**Goals:** Be the one who found something new · never look cheap or last-minute · buy it in
under five minutes from her phone.

**Frustrations:** The default options are the default for everyone — zero signal · she cannot
tell premium from packaging alone anymore · discovering a new brand and finding no price and no
reply in the DMs.

**What she needs:** Visible proof it is real and premium *before* she has to ask. A clear price.
A brand she can say the name of out loud without explaining it.

**Buys when:** the range looks like a considered collection, not an assortment, and the price is
on the page. **Hesitates when:** she has to DM to ask "how much" — that reads as either
expensive-and-hiding-it, or amateur.

**Channels:** Instagram Reels and Stories · WhatsApp forwards · Amman food accounts.

**How Rare Dates solves it:** Gives her the only thing the default gift can't — a gift that
starts a conversation instead of ending one.

---

### Hazem, 49 — Operations Director, mid-size firm, Abdoun

> *"I need two hundred boxes, they need to look serious, and I need them before Ramadan starts."*

**His world:** Signs off the seasonal client-gift budget every year. It is an annoying,
recurring, high-stakes errand: the gift represents the company, arrives on partners' and
clients' desks, and going cheap is noticed. He has used the same supplier for four years out of
inertia, not loyalty.

**Goals:** A gift that reflects well on the firm · one supplier, one invoice, delivered on
schedule · optional branding on the box or card.

**Frustrations:** Corporate options are either mass and generic or absurdly overpriced · nobody
confirms delivery dates in writing · suppliers who cannot handle 200 units without drama.

**What he needs:** Evidence of capacity, and a named person who answers. A price per unit at
volume. A confirmed delivery window.

**Buys when:** the brand looks like an operation rather than a hobby, and someone replies with
specifics the same day. **Hesitates when:** the site is clearly consumer-only with no bulk path.

**Channels:** LinkedIn peripherally, but the actual route is a referral plus a website he checks
once to confirm the brand is legitimate.

**How Rare Dates solves it:** He is the highest-ticket, most repeatable revenue in this market
and the least served by Instagram-native rivals — most cannot fulfil at volume. **Even under D3,
the site needs a visible corporate path.** One section, one enquiry CTA.

---

### Nadia, 54 — hosts everyone, Deir Ghbar

> *"When people come to my house, what I put on the table is my name."*

**Her world:** Hosts iftar several times each Ramadan and holds the family's standing Friday
lunch. Buys in quantity, in advance, and by relationship. Her daughters send her Instagram links;
she decides, then orders by WhatsApp — often by voice note.

**Goals:** A table that looks generous · something guests haven't seen before · a supplier she
can call directly next time.

**Frustrations:** Traditional houses feel tired · the new Instagram brands look pretty but she
doesn't know if they'll actually deliver on the day · she does not want to place an order
through a form.

**What she needs:** To feel she is dealing with a real, reachable person, and to be treated as a
returning customer, not a transaction.

**Buys when:** her daughter sends the link and it looks respectable · someone answers the voice
note. **Hesitates when:** ordering feels automated and impersonal.

**Channels:** WhatsApp above all · Instagram passively, via family.

**How Rare Dates solves it:** She is the reason the range must be legible **in Arabic, in full** —
not a translated afterthought. She is also the highest repeat-rate customer in the market and the
one most likely to hand the brand to ten other households.

### Watch segment — the diaspora carrier `[I]`

Jordanians in the Gulf, and visitors flying out of Queen Alia, who want a gift that is *from
Jordan*. Not a launch priority, but it argues for travel-safe packaging and a shelf-life
statement on the site. Cheap to accommodate now, expensive to retrofit.

## 04 — Audience Language

**How they describe the problem:**
- "Everyone brings the same thing."
- "بدي إشي مميز، مش عادي" — *I want something special, not the usual*
- "I don't want to arrive empty-handed but I don't want the usual box."
- "شو أجيب معي؟" — *what do I bring with me* — the actual search query in their head
- "It has to look expensive." / "بدها تكون شكلها فخمة"

**How they describe success:**
- "They asked me where I got it."
- "شكلها بتجنن" — *it looks stunning*
- "It looked like I actually thought about it."
- "I found something no one else has."

**How they justify choosing competitors:**
- "At least I know it'll arrive." — reliability beats novelty under time pressure
- "It's what everyone knows." — the safe choice is safe *because* it is generic
- "بعرفهم من زمان" — *I've known them for years*

**Brand-building implication:** the buyer's language is about **being seen giving**, not about
taste, ingredients, or health. Almost all category marketing talks about the product. Messaging
that talks about **the handover** will be the only thing in the feed addressing what the buyer is
actually thinking. `→ messaging.md`

## 05 — Who This Brand Is Not For

- **The everyday snacker.** Anyone buying dates by the kilo for the house. Wrong price, wrong
  frequency, and chasing them drags the brand toward volume and away from rarity.
- **The health buyer.** Sugar-free, keto, "natural energy", macros. The product is Belgian
  chocolate wrapped around a date — it is indulgence, and pretending otherwise is a losing fight
  fought on someone else's ground.
- **The bargain hunter.** Anyone who opens with "what's your best price". Discounting is the
  fastest way to kill a rarity claim — **a brand called *Rare* cannot run a sale.**
- **The supermarket shelf.** Being available everywhere is the literal opposite of the name. If
  it is in every hypermarket, the brand is over.

## 06 — Implications

**Messaging** — lead with the handover, not the ingredients. The product's job is to make the
giver look good. Ingredients are the *proof*, not the pitch. Under **D2** this matters more, not
less: text carries the promise, so the words must do the persuading the images cannot.

**Channel** — Instagram is the top of funnel, WhatsApp is the till, and the website is the
**credibility bridge** between them. Its job is to convert "is this real?" into "here's what I
want" in one scroll. That is a narrow, achievable job — and the reason D3 is right.

**Voice** — restrained, warm, certain. Never salesy, never a superlative without a fact behind it.
The existing copy rhythm already achieves this and should be codified rather than replaced. Arabic
must be *written*, not translated: Layan reads English, Nadia reads Arabic, and Nadia is the
repeat customer.

**Product and experience**
1. **Publish prices.** The single biggest conversion lever with Layan, and it costs nothing.
2. **A visible corporate path** even though D3 is consumer-first — Hazem is the margin.
3. **Reply speed is a brand attribute.** A slow WhatsApp reply damages the brand more than a
   mediocre image. Worth stating in the guidelines as a real, measurable standard.
4. **Build for the Ramadan spike.** Demand is seasonal and brutal; the site needs a seasonal slot
   designed in from day one, not bolted on in February.
5. **Full Arabic, RTL, first-class.** Not a toggle that leads to a broken layout.
