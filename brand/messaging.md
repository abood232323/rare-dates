# Brand Messaging — Rare Dates

Reads `positioning.md` · `audience.md` · `decisions.md`. Bilingual EN + AR per **D5** — Arabic is
**written, not translated**. Where the Arabic differs in meaning from the English, that is
deliberate and noted.

> visually. **The copy carries the entire persuasion burden.** Every line below is written to do
> work an image would normally do.

---

## 01 — Core Message

> **Rare Dates helps Jordanians who give often arrive with something that gets remembered, by
> making a deliberately short collection of Medjool dates finished in Belgian chocolate.**

Strategic anchor, not a public line. Everything below must ladder to it.

---

## 02 — Value Proposition

Rare Dates is a small Jordanian house making Medjool dates filled and finished in Belgian
chocolate — nine pieces, two collections, and nothing added to pad a menu. It is built for the
occasions Jordanians actually give for: Ramadan, Eid, an engagement, a new baby, a table you're
hosting.

Where the category splits between heritage houses that feel like your grandmother's gift and
imported chocolate that could have come from anywhere, Rare Dates is the modern one that is
still ours. **You are not buying dates. You are buying the question that follows the box being
opened.**

---

## 03 — Tagline

### Recommendation: keep the one you have.

**"Not Every Date Is Rare."** is already the strongest asset in this brand. It states the
category problem and the brand's answer in five words, puns on the name without being cheap,
and is impossible for a competitor to use. Replacing it would be destroying value. The options
below exist to prove that by comparison, not to displace it.

| # | Tagline | Style | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **"Not Every Date Is Rare."** ⭐ | Witty / functional | **RECOMMENDED — keep.** Owns the name, frames the category, invites the comparison the brand wins. Already in market. |
| 2 | "Some gifts get eaten. Ours get asked about." | Emotional | The sharpest expression of the position, but too long for a tagline. **Use it as the website hero headline instead** — that is its correct job. |
| 3 | "Rare by design." | Functional | Clean, states that scarcity is deliberate. Weaker: reads like a furniture brand and drops the category. |
| 4 | "The gift they remember." | Emotional | Warm, audience-true. Weaker: any gift brand could say it. Fails the ownability test. |
| 5 | "Nine, and no more." | Witty | Scarcity made concrete and confident. Real risk: it hard-codes the range and becomes a liability the day a tenth piece launches. Better as a campaign line than a tagline. |

**Arabic tagline:**

> **«ما كل تمرٍ نادر»**

Classical register, mirrors the English construction, and carries the same double reading. Avoid
the literal «ليس كل تمر نادر» — grammatical but flat, and it loses the cadence.

**Arabic wordmark:** «تمور نادرة» *(Tumūr Nādira)* — see `identity.md` for how it locks up with
the Latin mark.

## 04 — Messaging Hierarchy

### Level 1 — Brand Headline

> **EN:** Some gifts get eaten. Ours get asked about.
> **AR:** «كل الهدايا تُؤكل. هديّتنا يُسأل عنها.»

### Level 2 — Supporting Statement

> **EN:** Nine pieces. Medjool dates, filled and finished in Belgian chocolate, made in small
> batches in Jordan. Made for the occasions you actually give for.
>
> **AR:** «تسع قطع. تمر مجدول، محشوّ ومغلّف بالشوكولا البلجيكية، يُصنع بكميات قليلة في الأردن. لكل مناسبة تستحق.»

*Note the Arabic closes on «لكل مناسبة تستحق» — "for every occasion that deserves it." The English
closes on the giver; the Arabic closes on the occasion. That is the correct emphasis for each
reader, and it is why this is not a translation.*

### Level 3 — Key Messages

**① A short range, on purpose**
Nine pieces across two collections, and nothing added to fill a menu. Every new flavour has to
earn its place by displacing one that's already there.
*Proof:* 9 SKUs, 2 collections. Verifiable by looking at the range.

**② Named ingredients, not adjectives**
Medjool — the cultivar, not a generic "date". Belgian chocolate — an origin standard, not
"premium chocolate". Say the thing instead of describing it.
*Proof:* every product listing names its filling, its chocolate and its inclusions in full.

**③ From here** ⚠️ *conditional — see `positioning.md` §05 #6*
Jordan grows some of the best Medjool in the world, below sea level in the Jordan Valley.
*Proof:* Jordan Valley sourcing. **Do not publish this message until the client confirms it.**
If sourcing is imported, delete this pillar and promote ④ — the position survives without it.

**④ Built for the handover**
Designed around the two seconds that actually matter: the box changing hands, and the box being
opened. Presentation is not packaging here — it is the product.
*Proof:* gift-first box formats; the range is sold as collections, never loose by weight.

### Level 4 — Proof Point Bank

Pull from here whenever a claim needs backing. Never make a claim not on this list.

1. **Medjool** — the largest and most prized date cultivar. ✅
2. **Belgian chocolate** — named origin standard, dark and white. ✅
3. **Nine pieces, two collections.** Rarity is structural, not asserted. ✅
4. **A contemporary flavour set no heritage house offers** — Biscoff, matcha, red velvet,
   Raffaello, pomegranate, lemon. ✅
5. **Direct only.** Not in supermarkets, and not planned to be. ✅
6. **Jordan Valley Medjool.** ❓ confirm — highest-value claim available to the brand
7. **Small batch, made in Amman.** ❓ confirm
8. **A named maker behind it.** ❓ decide — founder-led converts better with Nadia than faceless

## 05 — Audience-Specific Messaging

### For Layan — the occasion gifter *(primary, volume)*
- **Cares most about:** not showing up with the same box as everyone else; not looking cheap.
- **Lead with:** the headline. *Some gifts get eaten. Ours get asked about.* Then the range,
  then the price.
- **Lands hardest:** proof 4 (flavours nobody else has), proof 3 (short range = you found
  something), visible pricing.
- **Avoid:** heritage language, tradition, anything that makes it sound like her mother's gift.

### For Hazem — corporate gifting *(secondary, margin)*
- **Cares most about:** the gift reflecting well on the firm; delivery on a date, in writing.
- **Lead with:** capability and seriousness. *Corporate gifting, from twenty boxes up.
  Branded cards. Confirmed delivery dates.*
- **Lands hardest:** proof 2 (named ingredients read as quality control), proof 5 (exclusivity
  = his clients haven't received it from someone else), a named human who replies.
- **Avoid:** cute copy, emoji, anything that reads as a hobby operation.

### For Nadia — the host *(secondary, repeat rate)*
- **Cares most about:** her table, her name, and dealing with a real person.
- **Lead with:** Arabic, warmth, occasion. «لكل مناسبة تستحق» / hosting, Ramadan, family.
- **Lands hardest:** proof 6 and 7 if confirmed — *made here, by hand* is decisive for her —
  plus a phone number that a person answers.
- **Avoid:** English-first anything, forms, automation, the word "order" where «طلب» is warmer.

## 06 — Messaging by Channel

| Channel | Headline approach | Tone | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Website hero** | The positioning line, full stop. No offer, no CTA stacking. | Restrained, certain | Short |
| **Product cards** | Flavour name + exactly what is inside. Facts, no adjectives. | Precise, plain | Very short |
| **Instagram bio** | Tagline + what it is + where + how to order. | Confident, minimal | Very short |
| **Instagram captions** | One thought per line. Never more than four lines. | Understated | Short |
| **WhatsApp first reply** | Named human + price + delivery, in the first message. | Warm, fast, specific | Short |
| **Corporate section** | Capability, minimums, lead time. | Straight, businesslike | Medium |
| **Packaging / gift card** | The tagline, or nothing. | Silent luxury | Very short |

### Instagram bio — replace the current one

```
Rare Dates · تمور نادرة
Not Every Date Is Rare.
Medjool dates in Belgian chocolate. Amman.
Order on WhatsApp ↓
```

Change from today: drops "Luxury Medjool Dates" (a category claim any competitor could make, per
`positioning.md` §08), drops "Launching Soon" the day the site is live, adds Arabic, and adds a
route to the till.

### WhatsApp opening reply — this is a brand touchpoint, treat it as one

Under **D3 the WhatsApp thread is the shop.** Reply speed and the first message do the work a
storefront would. Template:

> **EN:** Hi — thank you for reaching out. I'm [name] from Rare Dates.
> The [collection] box is [X] pieces at JOD [price]. We deliver across Amman, same day before
> 4pm. Which occasion is it for?
>
> **AR:** «أهلاً وسهلاً، معك [الاسم] من تمور نادرة.
> علبة [المجموعة] فيها [X] قطعة بسعر [السعر] دينار. التوصيل داخل عمّان بنفس اليوم قبل الساعة ٤.
> المناسبة شو؟»

Three things in the first message: a **name**, a **price**, a **delivery promise**. Then a
question that moves it forward. Never open with "Hello, how can I help you?" — that is a call
centre, not a luxury house.

## 07 — Things Not To Say

**Delete these from all copy — they are the credibility leak identified in the audit:**
- "Premium", "finest", "the best", "truly special", "highest quality" — unearned superlatives.
  **Replace each with a proof point or cut it.** Say *Belgian chocolate*, not *the finest
  chocolate*. The fact is more impressive than the adjective.
- "Luxury" as a self-description. Luxury brands do not call themselves luxury.
- "Handcrafted with love", "made with passion" — category wallpaper.

**Never say, on positioning grounds:**
- Any discount language — "sale", "offer", "% off", "limited time deal". A brand called *Rare*
  cannot discount. Seasonal *editions* are allowed; seasonal *prices* are not.
- Health framing — "guilt-free", "natural energy", "healthy treat", "sugar-free". Wrong ground.
- Gulf-borrowed cues — implied Emirati or Saudi heritage. It trades the only thing Bateel
  cannot copy.
- "Available everywhere", "now in stores nationwide".

**Never say, on honesty grounds — direct consequence of D2:**
- Do not describe the AI imagery as photography, and do not caption a render in a way that
  implies it is the specific box being sold.
- Do not claim awards, certifications, stockists or press the brand does not have.
- Do not claim a sourcing origin until it is confirmed (`positioning.md` §05 #6).

**Competitor-adjacent language to avoid:** "the world's finest dates" (Bateel's territory —
using it makes Rare Dates the imitation), "since [year]" heritage framing (Zalatimo's ground,
and false here), gift-box ritual language that echoes Patchi.
