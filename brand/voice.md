# Brand Voice — Rare Dates

Reads `positioning.md` · `messaging.md` · `audience.md`. Bilingual per **D5**.
Anyone writing for this brand — website, Instagram, WhatsApp, packaging — writes from this file.

---

## 01 — Voice Overview

Rare Dates speaks the way an expensive thing is handed over: quietly, and without explanation.
It states facts and lets the reader draw the conclusion, because a brand that has to tell you it
is luxurious has already lost the argument. It is warm but never familiar, certain but never
loud, and it would rather say one true sentence than three impressive ones. It knows its buyer
is standing in a shop deciding what to bring to a house, and it respects that this decision is
social, not culinary. Above all it is **short** — the restraint in the writing is the same
restraint as in the nine-piece range, and both are the product.

> **Voice essence:**
> *Rare Dates sounds like someone who knows exactly what they've made — precise, unhurried, and
> quietly certain.*

The existing Instagram copy already does most of this. This document codifies what is working
rather than replacing it.

## 02 — Tone Dimensions

**Formality — Casual ←———————•——→ Formal**
*Formal side, but not stiff.* Complete sentences, correct grammar, no slang and no internet
register. It is not chatty, but it is not corporate either — closer to a well-written menu than
to a press release.
> ✅ "Nine pieces. Two collections. Nothing else."
> ❌ "We've got 9 amazing flavors and honestly they're all incredible 🤩"

**Energy — Calm ←•———————————→ Energetic**
*Firmly calm.* The brand never sells hard, never rushes, never manufactures excitement. Energy
comes from the product being interesting, not from the writing being loud.
> ✅ "The Signature Collection is here."
> ❌ "IT'S FINALLY HERE!!! You asked, we delivered!!!"

**Humour — Serious ←————•—————→ Witty**
*Serious, with one dry note.* The brand does not do jokes. It does allow the single dry pun it
was built on — *Not Every Date Is Rare* — and nothing more. One wink per brand is elegant; two
is a personality.

**Expertise — Accessible ←———•————→ Authoritative**
*Authoritative but never lecturing.* It names things precisely — Medjool, not "dates"; Belgian,
not "premium" — and then stops. It does not explain what Medjool is unless asked. Assuming the
reader is knowledgeable is itself a form of respect, and it flatters the buyer.
> ✅ "Medjool dates, filled and finished in Belgian chocolate."
> ❌ "Medjool dates — known as the 'king of dates', these large, soft fruits are prized for..."

**Warmth — Distant ←————————•——→ Intimate**
*Warm, and warmer in Arabic.* English leans a half-step cooler and more precise; Arabic leans
warmer and speaks to the occasion and the guest. This is deliberate — see §09.

## 03 — Voice Qualities

### Precise
The brand names the actual thing. Cultivar, origin, filling, count. Precision is how a small
brand signals it knows what it's doing, and it is the only luxury signal available under **D2**.
- **Do:** "Roasted pistachio, blended into a smooth filling. Dark Belgian chocolate."
- **Don't:** "Made with the finest premium ingredients for an unforgettable taste experience."

### Restrained
Say less than you could. The pause after a short sentence does work that another sentence
would undo. If a line can lose a word, it loses the word.
- **Do:** "Nine pieces. That's the whole range."
- **Don't:** "We're proud to offer an exclusive curated selection of nine exquisite pieces."

### Certain
No hedging, no apologising, no asking for permission. The brand does not say "we hope you'll
love it" — it says what the thing is and lets that stand.
- **Do:** "This is the range. It doesn't change often."
- **Don't:** "We think you might really enjoy trying some of our flavours!"

### Grounded
It is from Amman, not from a mood board. It refers to real occasions by name — Ramadan, an
engagement, a table you're hosting — instead of abstract "special moments".
- **Do:** "For the iftar you're hosting on Thursday."
- **Don't:** "For life's most precious moments."

### Unhurried
No countdowns, no false scarcity, no "don't miss out". The brand's scarcity is real and
structural, so it never needs to perform urgency. Performing it would expose the real thing as
fake.
- **Do:** "Ramadan boxes open in January."
- **Don't:** "⏰ ONLY 3 LEFT — order NOW before they're gone!"

### Generous
It is a gifting brand. It should sound like someone who enjoys giving — in the choice of verbs
and in how customers are addressed. Warm, never transactional.
- **Do:** "Tell us the occasion and we'll put it together."
- **Don't:** "Please select your quantity and submit your order request."

## 04 — Vocabulary

### Words we use
rare · collection · piece · filled · finished · Medjool · Belgian · box · occasion · table ·
hosting · give · handed · small batch · short · chosen · dark · white · Amman · Jordan ·
«تمور» · «مجدول» · «مناسبة» · «ضيافة» · «علبة» · «تُهدى» · «قطعة»

### Words we avoid
premium · finest · luxury *(as self-description)* · exquisite · indulge · decadent · artisanal ·
handcrafted with love · curated · elevate · experience *(as a noun)* · journey · unforgettable ·
game-changing · must-try · yummy · delicious *(too small a word for this brand)* · treat ·
snack · guilt-free · healthy · natural energy · superfood · deal · offer · discount · sale ·
"hurry" · "don't miss out" · "click here"

### Brand-specific language
- The brand is **Rare Dates** — always both words, never "Rare" alone, never "RD".
- Arabic wordmark: **«تمور نادرة»**. Never transliterate as "Tumur Nadira" in running copy.
- A single unit is a **piece**, never a "product", "item", or "unit".
- A group of pieces is a **collection**, never a "range", "line", "assortment", or "selection box".
- Customers are addressed directly as **you**. Never "our valued customers", never "users",
  never "clients" (except in the corporate section, where "clients" is correct because it means
  *Hazem's* clients, not ours).
- The two collections keep their current names for now — **Signature** and **White Chocolate
  Fruit** — but see the note in §10.

## 05 — Writing Style Rules

**Sentence length** — Short by default. One idea per sentence, one thought per line. A medium
sentence is allowed when it carries a fact. Long sentences are for the story page only, never
for product copy or the hero.

**Line breaks** — The brand's existing Instagram rhythm is correct and should be kept: one
thought per line, a break between each. It creates the pause that restraint depends on.

**Punctuation** — Full stops, always. Em-dashes sparingly, for a beat. **Exclamation marks are
banned** — there is no sentence this brand needs to shout. Ellipses banned; they read as
hesitant, and this brand is certain. No emoji in website copy; at most one on Instagram, never
in the first line.

**Capitalisation** — Sentence case everywhere, including headlines. The one exception is the
wordmark **RARE DATES**, which is set in letterspaced caps as a *logo*, not as text. Never
write body copy in all caps. Never Title Case A Headline Like This.

**Numbers** — Spell out one to nine in running copy ("nine pieces"). Numerals for prices,
quantities over nine, dates and times ("JOD 24", "20 boxes", "before 4pm"). Prices always as
`JOD 24` — never "24 JD", never "24 dinars", never a bare number.

**Contractions** — Yes in English. "Doesn't", "we'll", "it's". They keep formality from tipping
into stiffness. No contractions in the corporate section.

**Voice** — Active. "We make", not "our products are made". Passive is allowed only when the
object genuinely matters more than the actor: *"filled and finished in Belgian chocolate."*

**Humour** — Off-limits, with the single licensed exception of the tagline pun.

**Arabic** — Written, not translated. See §09.

## 06 — Channel Adaptations

Voice is constant. Tone flexes.

| Channel | Tone adjustment | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Website hero** | Coolest and most restrained. Says one thing. | "Some gifts get eaten. Ours get asked about." |
| **Product cards** | Pure fact. Zero persuasion — the specificity persuades. | "Matcha white chocolate. Japanese matcha in a silky white chocolate filling." |
| **Story / about page** | Warmest and longest. Only place sentences may run. | "It started with the same problem everyone has in Amman." |
| **Instagram captions** | Restrained, four lines maximum. | "The Signature Collection.\nNine pieces.\nAmman.\nOrder on WhatsApp." |
| **WhatsApp** | Warmest and fastest. A named human, not a brand. | "Hi — I'm Wajih from Rare Dates. The Signature box is 16 pieces, JOD 24." |
| **Corporate section** | Most formal. No contractions, no wit. | "Corporate orders from 20 boxes. Branded cards available. Confirmed delivery dates." |
| **Errors / empty states** | Plain and brief. Never cute, never apologetic twice. | "That page doesn't exist. The collections are here." |

## 07 — Before and After

**Before**
> ✨ Introducing our AMAZING new Signature Collection!! 🤩 Made with the FINEST premium Medjool
> dates and the most delicious Belgian chocolate. You're going to LOVE these! 😍 DM us now!!

**After**
> The Signature Collection.
> Six pieces. Medjool dates, filled and finished in Belgian chocolate.
> Order on WhatsApp.

*Why it works:* every superlative is replaced by a fact, and the facts are more convincing than
the adjectives were.

---

**Before**
> We are proud to offer a curated selection of luxury artisanal dates, handcrafted with passion
> and love to elevate your gifting experience.

**After**
> Nine pieces, made in Amman. Built for the occasions you actually give for.

*Why it works:* drops four category clichés and replaces them with a place and a purpose.

---

**Before**
> 🔥 RAMADAN SALE! 20% OFF all boxes! Limited time only — don't miss out!!

**After**
> Ramadan boxes open in January.
> They're made to order, so earlier is better.

*Why it works:* the urgency is real (production capacity) instead of manufactured, and it
doesn't discount — which the name forbids.

---

**Before**
> Hello! Thank you for contacting Rare Dates. How may we assist you today? 😊

**After**
> Hi — I'm Wajih from Rare Dates.
> The Signature box is 16 pieces, JOD 24. We deliver across Amman same day before 4pm.
> Which occasion is it for?

*Why it works:* under **D3 the WhatsApp thread is the shop.** A name, a price and a delivery
promise in the first message do the work a storefront would. "How may we assist you" is a call
centre.

---

**Before**
> Our dates are simply the best you'll ever taste — an unforgettable experience in every bite!

**After**
> Medjool dates. Dark Belgian chocolate. Roasted pistachio.

*Why it works:* the brand cannot prove taste in writing, and claiming it invites disagreement.
Naming the components lets the reader do the imagining, and they will always imagine better than
the copy could promise. **This is the single most important rewrite pattern in this document.**

## 08 — Things We Never Say

- **Superlatives without a fact behind them.** "Finest", "best", "premium", "world-class",
  "unforgettable". If it cannot be verified by reading a label, it does not get written.
- **"Luxury", about ourselves.** Luxury brands never do.
- **Any discount language.** Sale, offer, % off, deal, promo, "limited time". *A brand called
  Rare cannot discount.* Seasonal editions yes; seasonal prices no.
- **Manufactured urgency.** Countdowns, "only X left", "hurry". The scarcity is real; faking it
  would expose the real one as fake too.
- **Health claims.** Guilt-free, healthy, natural energy, superfood, sugar-free.
- **Over-apologising.** Say what happened and what happens next. Apologise once, briefly, never
  twice in one message.
- **Borrowed heritage.** "Since 1860" framing, implied Gulf origin, "ancient tradition". The
  brand is new and Jordanian — both are advantages, and pretending otherwise forfeits them.
- **Anything implying an AI render is a photograph** of the specific box being sold — direct
  consequence of **D2**. Never write "pictured here" or "as shown" next to a render.
- **Emoji in the first line** of anything, ever.

## 09 — Arabic Voice

Arabic is not a translation layer. It has its own register, and often its own emphasis.

**Register** — Modern Standard Arabic for anything written and permanent: the website, packaging,
the wordmark. **Levantine Jordanian colloquial for WhatsApp**, where MSA would sound like a
government letter. The gap between these two registers is the single most common failure in
Jordanian brand copy — MSA in a chat is cold, colloquial on packaging is cheap.

**Emphasis differs by design.** English addresses the *giver* — *you found something*. Arabic
addresses the *occasion and the guest* — «لكل مناسبة تستحق». Both are correct for their reader.
When the two versions of a line say slightly different things, that is the system working, not
a translation error.

**Never machine-translate.** Particularly: "collection" is «مجموعة», not «تشكيلة» (retail-coded);
"piece" is «قطعة»; "order" as a verb is «اطلب», warmer than «اشترِ».

**Typography and layout** — full RTL, not a mirrored English page. Arabic sets larger than Latin
at the same optical size and needs more line-height. Numerals: Western Arabic numerals (1، 2، 3)
for prices, since that is what Jordanian commerce uses. See `identity.md`.

**Never mix scripts inside a sentence.** «تمر Medjool» is wrong — use «تمر مجدول».

## 10 — Open Notes for the Client

1. **"Signature Collection" is a generic name** and every brand uses it. Renaming it is a real
   opportunity — not urgent, but worth deciding before packaging is printed. Same for
   "White Chocolate Fruit Collection", which is a description rather than a name.
2. **Who signs the WhatsApp messages?** The voice above assumes a named human. Confirm the name —
   it appears in every first reply and on the corporate section, and it is the warmest asset a
   small brand has.
