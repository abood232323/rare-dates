## Audience
- **Primary Audience** `[I]`: Jordanian gift-buyers, 25–55, mid-to-upper income, Amman-centred.
  They buy for an occasion, not for themselves. Three buying modes:
  1. **Occasion gifting** — Ramadan, Eid, weddings, engagements, new babies, condolences,
     hosting guests. The dominant volume driver in this market.
  2. **Corporate gifting** — companies, banks, clinics, hotels sending seasonal gifts.
     Higher ticket, repeat, low marketing cost. Historically underserved by IG-native brands.
  3. **Self-indulgence / hosting** — a smaller everyday-luxury basket.
- **Key Problem** `[I]`: Dates are the default Jordanian gift and therefore say nothing.
  A buyer who wants a gift that reads as *considered* is stuck choosing between traditional
  date boxes (safe, generic, invisible) and imported chocolate (expensive, not local, not
  personal). Nothing in between reads as both premium and rooted here.
- **Their Language** `[I]`: EN — "gift box", "corporate gifting", "Medjool", "Belgian chocolate",
  "presentation", "something different". AR — «هدية», «علبة», «ضيافة», «تمر مجدول», «فخم»,
  «رمضان», «عيد», «شي مميز». Note the AR buyer talks about the *occasion* and the *guest*;
  the EN buyer talks about the *product* and the *ingredients*. Bilingual copy must not be
  a straight translation — see `voice.md` when written.

## Positioning
- **Differentiation** `[I]`: The category splits into heritage date houses (traditional,
  ceremonial, aimed at an older buyer) and imported chocolate gifting (premium but foreign,
  no local meaning). Rare Dates can own the gap: **a modern luxury built on a Jordanian
  staple** — the date as the hero, treated with the craft and restraint of fine chocolate.
  The flavour range (Biscoff, matcha, red velvet, Raffaello) already signals a younger,
  contemporary buyer than any heritage house serves. That is the real wedge, and it is
  currently accidental rather than deliberate.
- **Competitors** `[I] [?]` — the exact Jordanian set must be verified in `competitor-branding`:
  - *Bateel* — the global luxury-date benchmark; sets the ceiling for what "luxury dates" looks like
  - *Zalatimo Sweets* — Jordanian heritage incumbent, owns the traditional gifting occasion
  - *Patchi* — Levant luxury chocolate gifting; owns the gift-box ritual
  - Amman's Instagram-native artisanal date/chocolate makers — the real near-term rivals
- **Market Position**: Premium / accessible-luxury, gift-led `[I]`

## Brand Personality
- **Personality Words** `[I]`, read off the existing tone: *rare, restrained, precise,
  indulgent, quietly confident*. The brand does not shout; it understates and lets the
  product carry. The current copy already does this well.
- **Tone**: Formal-leaning but not stiff. Serious, never playful. Short declarative
  sentences, one thought per line. Confident without hype. `[E]`
- **Voice Admires** `[?]`: unconfirmed — ask the client which brands he looks up to.
  The existing copy pattern most resembles Bateel and fine-chocolate maisons.

## Values & Mission
> All `[?]` — drafted from evidence so there is something concrete to react to.
> These must be confirmed or replaced by the client before `brand-guidelines`.
- **Core Values** (draft):
  1. **Rarity over volume** — a short, deliberate range; nothing made to pad a menu.
  2. **Real craft** — named ingredients, honest sourcing, no unearned superlatives.
  3. **Restraint** — in flavour, in packaging, in language. Luxury is what is left out.
  4. **Rooted** — Jordanian by origin and intent, not a copy of a Gulf brand.
- **Mission** (draft): To take the most ordinary gift in Jordan and make it the most
  considered one.

## Assets worth keeping (do not redesign these)
1. **"Not Every Date Is Rare."** — a genuinely strong line. It states the category
   problem and the brand's answer in five words, and it puns on the name without being
   cheap. This should become the brand's spine, not just a bio line. `[E]`
2. **Wine + gold palette.** Correct territory for the category, warmer and more
   distinctive than the black-and-gold default. Needs documenting, not replacing. `[E]`
3. **The copy rhythm.** Short, restrained, one-thought-per-line. Already on-brand. `[E]`
4. **A defined 9-SKU range across two collections.** Most pre-launch brands do not
   have this much product clarity. `[E]`

## Defects on record (these are what "semi brand" means here)
1. **Two competing logos in simultaneous use** — ornamental script + palm tree vs.
   minimal vesica + Trajan caps. They read as two different companies. Must be resolved
   before anything else is designed. `[E]`
2. **No documented palette, type, or usage rules** — so every new asset drifts. `[E]`
3. **Unearned superlatives** — "premium", "finest", "truly special" with no proof
   points behind them. Luxury is proven, not asserted. `[E]`
4. **AI-generated product imagery, publicly labelled by Instagram as "AI content"** —
   directly contradicts the craft claim and cannot carry a launch. See
   `assets/source-evidence.md`. `[E]`
5. **Generic collection names** — "Signature Collection" is what every brand calls its
   range. `[E]`
6. **No Arabic identity yet** — a bilingual Jordanian luxury brand with no Arabic
   wordmark, no Arabic typeface, no RTL thinking. `[E]`

## Open with client — blocking `brand-guidelines`, not blocking the next steps
- **Which logo is the real one?** (My read: Mark B, the vesica + serif caps. It is the
  one that survives at small sizes, embosses on packaging, and looks like the price point.)
- **Is a real photo shoot possible before launch?** This is the highest-leverage
  question in the whole project.
- **What is the 12-month goal** — retail volume, corporate accounts, a boutique, or export?
- **Selling channel at launch** — Instagram DM, WhatsApp, full e-commerce, or pickup?
- **Price point per box**, and is there a tiered range?
- **Who is "he"** — founder-led story available, or does the brand stay faceless?
