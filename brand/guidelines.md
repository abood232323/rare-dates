**Version 1.0 · 21 August 2026 · تمور نادرة**

## About This Guide

This is the operating manual for Rare Dates. It consolidates every brand decision made — strategic,
visual and verbal — into one reference so a designer, copywriter, printer or agency can produce
on-brand work without asking a question.

It is written for anyone making something with the brand's name on it. Read Part 1 once. Work from
Parts 2–5 daily.

**Where things live.** This document is the summary. The reasoning behind every decision sits in
the full package:

# PART 1 — FOUNDATION

## Brand Essence

**Mission**
To take the most ordinary gift in Jordan and make it the most considered one. ❓ *draft — confirm*

**Vision**
To be the gift Jordanians reach for when the occasion actually matters — and the one people ask
about after the box is opened. ❓ *draft — confirm*

**Values** ❓ *draft — confirm*
1. **Rarity over volume** — a short, deliberate range. Nothing is made to pad a menu.
2. **Real craft** — named ingredients, honest sourcing, no unearned superlatives.
3. **Restraint** — in flavour, in packaging, in language. Luxury is what is left out.
4. **Rooted** — Jordanian by origin and intent, not an imitation of a Gulf brand.

**Personality**
Rare · Restrained · Precise · Indulgent · Quietly confident

**Positioning statement**
> For Jordanians who give often and refuse to give the ordinary, **Rare Dates** is the modern
> Jordanian gift — Medjool dates finished in Belgian chocolate — that gets asked about, because
> the range is short by design, contemporary in a category that is not, and made here rather than
> imported.

**In one sentence**
> Rare Dates is the modern Jordanian gift — a short collection of Medjool dates in Belgian
> chocolate, for people who refuse to give the ordinary.

**The one strategic idea, if you remember nothing else**
> **Rare Dates competes in gifting, not in dates.** The date is the medium. The gift is the
> product. The moment of handing it over is what the brand owns.

# PART 2 — VISUAL IDENTITY

## Logo

**Primary mark:** a gold line-drawn vesica — the date-fruit form, two arcs meeting at a point top
and bottom, with a single interior vertical line — set above **RARE DATES** in letterspaced Roman
capitals (Cinzel, tracking `0.20em`).

> **The ornamental palm-tree mark in the burgundy circle is retired** (`decisions.md` D1). It must
> not appear on any new asset, and must be removed from the Instagram avatar and the carousel
> watermark. Two marks in circulation means two brands.

### Approved variations — four, and no others

| Variation | Composition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Vesica above wordmark, stacked, centred | Hero, packaging lid, anything with room |
| **Horizontal** | Vesica left, wordmark right | Site header, email signature, letterhead |
| **Mark only** | Vesica alone | Favicon, Instagram avatar, embossing, pattern |
| **Bilingual** | Vesica above `RARE DATES` + «تمور نادرة», hairline rule between | Packaging, Arabic site, all Jordanian retail |

**Clear space:** the full height of the vesica on all four sides. Nothing enters it.

**Minimum size:** primary lockup **120px / 28mm** wide · mark only **16px / 8mm**.

### Never
- [ ] Never stretch, condense, rotate or re-space the mark
- [ ] Never apply a gold gradient, bevel, sheen, glow or drop shadow — **flat gold only**; foil is
      a print finish, never a screen effect
- [ ] Never recolour outside `gold-500`, `cream` or `ink`
- [ ] Never place the lockup inside a circle or badge
- [ ] Never set the wordmark in any face other than Cinzel, or at tracking below `0.18em`
- [ ] Never put the mark on a busy image without a wine or ink scrim behind it
- [ ] Never reintroduce the palm tree
- [ ] Never use the full lockup below 120px — use the mark-only variation instead

## Colour

**Two hues. No third colour is ever introduced.** Flavour differentiation happens through imagery
and typography — never by giving a product its own colour. That discipline is what makes nine
products look like one collection.

### Primary

| Name | Hex | RGB | CMYK | Pantone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wine 700** *(the brand colour)* | `#4A0E1F` | 74 · 14 · 31 | 0 · 81 · 58 · 71 | 504 C ❓ verify on press |
| **Gold 500** | `#C9A227` | 201 · 162 · 39 | 0 · 19 · 81 · 21 | 4515 C printed · **872 C for foil** ❓ |

### Secondary — the wine ramp

| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Wine 900 | `#170509` | Vignette edges, gradient falloff |
| Wine 800 | `#2B0812` | Section grounds, cards on dark |
| Wine 600 | `#5C1327` | Elevated surfaces, hover |
| Wine 500 | `#74192F` | Borders, dividers, active |
| Gold 600 | `#9A7B1C` | Pressed states, gold on light |
| Gold 400 | `#E0C25C` | Hover, highlight |

### Neutrals

| Name | Hex | RGB | CMYK | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#0D0709` | 13 · 7 · 9 | 0 · 46 · 15 · 95 | Warm near-black. **Never pure `#000`.** |
| Cream | `#F2E9DC` | 242 · 233 · 220 | 0 · 4 · 9 · 5 | **All body text on dark.** |
| Cream muted | `#B8A894` | 184 · 168 · 148 | — | Secondary text, captions |
| Paper | `#FAF6F0` | 250 · 246 · 240 | — | The one light ground — long-form only |

### Usage rules
- **Dark by default.** Wine or ink grounds, cream text. `paper` appears on the story page and the
  corporate one-pager only.
- **Gold is punctuation, not paint.** The mark, headings, hairline rules, one CTA per screen.
  Past ~10% of a screen, gold has become decoration — cut it back.
- ⚠️ **Contrast, non-negotiable.** `gold-500` on `wine-700` is ≈**4.3:1** — fine for headings 24px+
  and for the mark, **and it fails for body text.** Body copy is always `cream` on wine (≈13:1).
  This is the rule most likely to be broken under deadline.
- **Never:** gold text on gold · cream on `gold-400` · pure white anywhere — white is colder than
  this brand and makes the wine read purple.
- **Never a flat fill.** Grounds are built from layered radial gradients plus a 3–5% SVG grain
  layer. Flat wine bands and looks like a template; a bloom looks like light on velvet.
- **Shadows are wine-tinted and layered**, never neutral grey:
  `0 1px 2px rgba(23,5,9,.4), 0 8px 24px rgba(23,5,9,.35), 0 24px 64px rgba(23,5,9,.25)`

## Typography

All four faces are on Google Fonts, free for commercial use.

| Role | Face | Weights | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | **Cinzel** | 400 | Caps only. Trajan-derived. |
| Display / headline | **Cormorant Garamond** | 300 · 400 | Never bold. |
| Body / UI | **Jost** | 300 · 400 · 500 | Geometric sans, Futura lineage. |
| Arabic display | **Reem Kufi** | 400 · 500 | Geometric Kufic. |
| Arabic body | **Tajawal** | 300 · 400 · 500 | |

> **Cinzel and Reem Kufi are both derived from letters carved in stone.** That shared inscriptional
> origin is the concept holding the bilingual system together — the two scripts are matched by how
> they were made, not by how they look.

> **Deliberately not Playfair Display** — the default "elegant serif" of every template online.
> Using it puts the brand in exactly the bucket it is trying to escape.

### Hierarchy

| Level | Face | Weight | Size (desktop) | Tracking | Line-height |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | Cinzel | 400 | contextual | `0.20em` | 1 |
| H1 / hero | Cormorant Garamond | 300 | 64–88px | `-0.03em` | 1.05 |
| H2 / section | Cormorant Garamond | 400 | 36–44px | `-0.02em` | 1.15 |
| H3 / product | Cinzel | 400 | 18–22px | `0.10em` | 1.3 |
| Body | Jost | 300 | 16–18px | `0.01em` | **1.7** |
| Caption | Jost | 300 | 14px | `0.01em` | 1.6 |
| Label / price | Jost | 500 | 11–13px | `0.14em` caps | 1.4 |
| Arabic H1 | Reem Kufi | 400 | 56–76px | `0` | 1.35 |
| Arabic body | Tajawal | 300 | 17–19px | `0` | **1.9** |

**Arabic sets larger and looser than Latin** — body ~1px up, line-height ~`+0.2`. A page that
mirrors the Latin scale looks cramped to an Arabic reader.

**Fallback stacks**
`Cormorant Garamond, 'Times New Roman', Georgia, serif` · `Jost, 'Century Gothic', Futura, system-ui, sans-serif` · `Cinzel, 'Trajan Pro', Georgia, serif` · `Tajawal, 'Segoe UI', Tahoma, sans-serif`

**Never:** slab serifs · humanist sans (Open Sans, Lato, Source Sans) · Inter or Roboto ·
condensed faces · scripts · Playfair Display · mixed scripts inside one sentence («تمر Medjool» ✗ →
«تمر مجدول» ✓).

## Imagery

**Aesthetic:** low-key still life. One raking light from the upper left, falling off fast into wine
shadow. Velvet and matte stone. Closer to a jewellery catalogue than a food blog.

**Subjects, in priority order:** ① the box — closed, held, being handed over ② material and texture
③ product at catalogue scale.

**Treatment:** warm-shifted, deep shadows. A `mix-blend-multiply` wine layer at 15–25% over every
image so the library reads as one world. `linear-gradient` from `#170509`/70 on anything carrying text.

**Fixed ratios:** `4:5` product · `16:9` lifestyle · `1:1` collection tiles.

### ⚠️ Rules that follow from D2 — AI imagery
The brand launched with AI-generated product imagery, risk stated and accepted. Until a real shoot
happens:
- No full-bleed photoreal hero render presented as the product. The hero leads with type.
- Product imagery at **catalogue scale** (≤480px in a card), never appetite close-up.
- **Every product card states the real spec in text. Text carries the promise; the image carries
  the mood.**
- Heavy art direction so images read as brand world, not as a photograph of this specific box.
- Never caption a render "pictured" or "as shown".
- Build image slots at fixed ratios so a real shoot drops in with zero rework.

**Avoid:** white seamless backgrounds · overhead flat-lays with scattered props · hands offering a
piece to camera · steam, splashes, mid-air drizzle · confetti bokeh · the current carousels'
floating-ingredients treatment, which reads supermarket promotion rather than luxury house.

## Iconography

Hairline line icons: stroke `1.25px`, round caps, 24px grid, gold or cream, never filled, never in
a circle. Permitted set: WhatsApp, Instagram, location, clock, box, arrow. Nothing else.

**The vesica is the only branded shape** — reusable as a bullet, divider, section marker, or
packaging pattern at 6–8% opacity. One shape, everywhere, never redrawn.

# PART 3 — VERBAL IDENTITY

## Voice Essence

> *Rare Dates sounds like someone who knows exactly what they've made — precise, unhurried, and
> quietly certain.*

## Tone

- **Formal, not stiff.** Complete sentences, no slang, no internet register. A well-written menu,
  not a press release.
- **Calm, never energetic.** The brand never sells hard. Interest comes from the product, not from
  volume.
- **Serious, with one dry note.** No jokes. The single licensed wink is the tagline pun.
- **Authoritative, never lecturing.** Name things precisely, then stop. Don't explain what Medjool
  is — assuming the reader knows is a form of respect.
- **Warm, and warmer in Arabic.**

## Voice qualities

| Quality | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Precise** | "Roasted pistachio, blended into a smooth filling. Dark Belgian chocolate." | "Made with the finest premium ingredients." |
| **Restrained** | "Nine pieces. That's the whole range." | "An exclusive curated selection of nine exquisite pieces." |
| **Certain** | "This is the range. It doesn't change often." | "We think you might really enjoy trying some!" |
| **Grounded** | "For the iftar you're hosting on Thursday." | "For life's most precious moments." |
| **Unhurried** | "Ramadan boxes open in January." | "⏰ ONLY 3 LEFT — order NOW!" |

## Writing style

**Sentences** short, one idea each, one thought per line. **Exclamation marks banned.** Ellipses
banned. **Sentence case everywhere**, including headlines — the only caps are the wordmark and
labels. Spell out one to nine; numerals for prices, quantities over nine, dates and times. Prices
always **`JOD 24`** — never "24 JD", never "24 dinars". Contractions yes in English, none in the
corporate section. Active voice. No emoji in website copy, at most one on Instagram, never in the
first line.

**The single most important rewrite pattern:**
> ✗ "Our dates are simply the best you'll ever taste."
> ✓ "Medjool dates. Dark Belgian chocolate. Roasted pistachio."
>
> The brand cannot prove taste in writing, and claiming it invites disagreement. Name the
> components and the reader imagines better than the copy could promise.

## Arabic register

**MSA** for anything written and permanent — website, packaging, wordmark.
**Levantine Jordanian colloquial** for WhatsApp, where MSA reads like a government letter.
Mixing these up is the most common failure in Jordanian brand copy.

Arabic is **written, not translated**. English addresses the giver; Arabic addresses the occasion
and the guest. Where the two versions differ in emphasis, that is the system working.
«مجموعة» not «تشكيلة» · «قطعة» · «اطلب» not «اشترِ».

## Vocabulary

**We say:** rare · collection · piece · filled · finished · Medjool · Belgian · box · occasion ·
table · hosting · give · handed · small batch · short · chosen · Amman · «مناسبة» · «ضيافة» ·
«علبة» · «تُهدى»

**We don't say:** premium · finest · luxury *(about ourselves)* · exquisite · indulge · decadent ·
artisanal · handcrafted with love · curated · elevate · experience · journey · unforgettable ·
must-try · delicious · treat · snack · guilt-free · healthy · superfood · deal · offer · discount ·
sale · "hurry" · "don't miss out"

**Naming:** always **Rare Dates**, both words — never "Rare", never "RD". Arabic: «تمور نادرة»,
never transliterated in running copy. A unit is a **piece**. A group is a **collection**. Customers
are **you** — never "our valued customers", never "users".

# PART 4 — MESSAGING

**Core message** *(internal anchor)*
> Rare Dates helps Jordanians who give often arrive with something that gets remembered, by making
> a deliberately short collection of Medjool dates finished in Belgian chocolate.

**Tagline** — **"Not Every Date Is Rare."** · AR: **«ما كل تمرٍ نادر»**
Locked. It is the strongest asset the brand owns — it states the category problem and the answer in
five words and cannot be used by a competitor. Use on packaging, the gift card, the Instagram bio,
and the site footer. It is a tagline, not a headline.

**Hero headline** — "Some gifts get eaten. Ours get asked about."
AR: «كل الهدايا تُؤكل. هديّتنا يُسأل عنها.»

**Key messages**
1. **A short range, on purpose** — nine pieces, two collections. Every new flavour must displace one.
2. **Named ingredients, not adjectives** — Medjool the cultivar; Belgian the origin standard.
3. **From here** ✅ — Jordan Valley Medjool. Confirmed 4 Sep 2026. Published.
4. **Built for the handover** — designed around the box changing hands and being opened.

**Proof bank** — never make a claim that isn't on this list.
✅ Medjool cultivar · ✅ Belgian chocolate · ✅ nine pieces / two collections · ✅ a contemporary
flavour set no heritage house offers · ✅ direct only, never a supermarket
✅ **Jordan Valley Medjool (confirmed 4 Sep 2026)** · ❓ small batch, made in Amman · ❓ a named maker

# PART 5 — BRAND IN USE

## Website
Dark wine ground, generous vertical rhythm, one gold CTA per screen. Cinzel wordmark, Cormorant
display, Jost body. Full RTL Arabic — a mirrored *layout*, not a mirrored English page. Every
interactive element gets hover, `focus-visible` and active states. Animate `transform` and
`opacity` only, spring easing, **never `transition-all`**. Grain over all full-bleed dark sections.
Prices published — per `audience.md`, the single biggest conversion lever available.

## Instagram
**Avatar: the Mark B vesica on ink, mark only.** This is the fastest visible win in the project —
one asset, an hour's work, and it retires the old mark.

Bio:
```
Rare Dates · تمور نادرة
Not Every Date Is Rare.
Medjool dates in Belgian chocolate. Amman.
Order on WhatsApp ↓
```
Grid alternates product → typographic → texture. Never three product posts in a row. Captions
four lines maximum.

## WhatsApp — this is the shop, not a support channel
Under `decisions.md` D3 the WhatsApp thread is where the sale happens. **Reply speed is a brand
attribute**, and a slow reply damages the brand more than a mediocre image.

Every first reply contains three things — **a name, a price, a delivery promise** — then a question
that moves it forward:
> Hi — I'm [name] from Rare Dates. The Signature box is [X] pieces, JOD [price]. We deliver across
> Amman same day before 4pm. Which occasion is it for?

Never open with "Hello, how can I help you?" That is a call centre.

## Packaging
Matte wine board, gold foil **debossed** — pressed into the lid, catching light rather than printed.
Interior in ink. Bilingual lockup. Gift card carries the tagline and nothing else. Wine tissue, gold
vesica sticker as the seal — **the customer breaks the mark to open the box, and that is the moment
the brand is remembered.** After the mark itself, packaging is the highest-leverage spend.

## Print and corporate
Wine stock, gold foil, Jost 300. The corporate one-pager is the one place `paper` ground is allowed
so it survives photocopying. No contractions, no wit, no emoji.

## Brand Don'ts — the checklist

- [ ] Never use the full lockup below **120px** — use mark-only
- [ ] Never let the retired palm-tree mark appear on anything
- [ ] Never use a colour outside the palette, or introduce a third hue
- [ ] Never set gold body text on wine — **cream only** (contrast)
- [ ] Never use a flat colour fill without gradient + grain
- [ ] Never use a font outside Cinzel / Cormorant / Jost / Reem Kufi / Tajawal
- [ ] Never write a superlative that isn't backed by the proof bank
- [ ] **Never discount.** A brand called *Rare* cannot run a sale
- [ ] Never make a health claim
- [ ] Never borrow Gulf-luxury or heritage-since-18XX cues
- [ ] Never present an AI render as a photograph of the specific box sold
- [ ] Never use `transition-all`, and never animate anything but transform and opacity
- [ ] Never let Arabic be a machine translation of the English
