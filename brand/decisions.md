## D1 · Primary mark — RESOLVED 2026-08-21
**Mark B is the identity.** Gold line-drawn vesica / date-fruit outline above
"RARE DATES" in wide-letterspaced classical serif caps.

- **Mark A (palm tree + ornamental script in burgundy circle) is retired.**
  It must be removed from: the Instagram avatar, the carousel watermark, and every
  future asset. Leaving it in circulation re-creates the two-brand problem.
- Rationale: survives at small sizes, embosses on packaging, matches the price point,
  and reads modern — consistent with a range built on Biscoff, matcha and red velvet
  rather than a heritage sweets house.
- Consequence: the existing profile avatar must be replaced before the site goes live,
  or the site and the Instagram will not look like the same company.

## D2 · Product imagery — DECIDED, RISK ACCEPTED 2026-08-21
**Launching with AI-generated product imagery. No photo shoot before launch.**

Client decision, made with the risk stated. Recorded here so it is not re-litigated,
and so the constraint is designed around rather than ignored.

Risk accepted (documented, not resolved):
- Instagram publicly labels the posts "AI content" directly beneath the brand name,
  next to caption copy claiming "crafted" and "finest".
- Rendered product does not depict what the customer receives — a consumer-protection
  and complaints exposure in Jordan.
- Press, stockists, hotels and corporate buyers will not transact off AI renders.

**Binding design rules that follow from D2** — these apply to the website:
1. No full-bleed photoreal hero render presented as the actual product. The hero
   leads with typography, the mark, and the wine/gold material world.
2. Product imagery appears at **catalogue scale**, not appetite-close-up scale.
   Small enough to communicate flavour, not large enough to make a promise.
3. Every product card carries the real, verifiable spec in text — filling, chocolate
   type, inclusions. **Text carries the promise; the image only carries the mood.**
4. Heavy art direction — wine grounds, gold, grain, gradient overlays — so images
   read as *brand world*, not as *photography of this specific box*.
5. Build the image slots so a real shoot drops straight in later with zero rework.
   Same aspect ratios, same treatment layer.
6. No claim on the site that cannot survive a customer holding the box.

**Revisit trigger:** the first real photo shoot, or the first customer complaint that
the product did not match the pictures — whichever comes first.

## D3 · Website job — RESOLVED 2026-08-21
**Catalogue + WhatsApp ordering.** Full brand showcase and complete product range;
orders transact through WhatsApp / Instagram DM. No cart, no checkout, no payment.

- Matches how premium Jordanian food brands actually sell, and is fastest to launch.
- Primary CTA sitewide: **order on WhatsApp**, pre-filled with the product context.
- Secondary CTA: corporate & bulk gifting enquiry (the highest-ticket repeat revenue
  in this market — worth a dedicated section even though it is not the site's main job).
- Implication: the site must do all the persuading, because there is no cart to
  recover an undecided visitor. Copy and range clarity carry the conversion.

## D5 · Languages — bilingual EN + AR
Arabic is a first-class identity, not a translation layer. Requires an Arabic wordmark,
an Arabic typeface paired to the Latin serif, and full RTL layout on the site.

## D6 · Sourcing — CONFIRMED 2026-09-04
**The Medjool is grown in the Jordan Valley.**

This was the highest-value open question in the project (`positioning.md` §05 #6) and
the answer is the strongest proof point the brand has. It converts "Jordanian" from
an address into a fact about the product, and it is the one claim Bateel and Patchi
can never make.

Consequences:
- It is stated on the homepage, in the gifting section and on every flavour page.
- Product listings say "Jordan Valley Medjool", not "Medjool date".
- It replaces the deleted superlatives as the brand's proof of quality.
